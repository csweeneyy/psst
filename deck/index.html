01 / Psst

Psst

An accountability app where the notifications are the product: they arrive at the right moment, in your own words, and take one tap to answer.

Native Swift 6 on iOS 26. Three notification tiers, an on device scheduler, a plain language assistant, and a Cloudflare Worker that holds the conversation.

02 / The problem

A checklist behind an app icon you have to remember to open

  • Most habit apps make the reminder a row in a list. The list waits for you, which is the exact thing you already failed to do.
  • Notification action buttons need a long press, and only the first two render. No public API changes this.
  • So the fastest route to "done" runs through launching the app. A nudge that costs a launch costs more than ignoring it.
  • A plain banner does not break Focus and does not break silent. If you asked to be held to something, it never arrives.

03 / The thesis

The notification is the product

A habit is a notification schedule. Every surface in the app exists to make answering that notification take one tap from wherever you already are: the Lock Screen, the Home Screen, the Action button.

One entry point. Every path that puts a nudge in front of you goes through NudgeDelivery.
Picking a tier is the product. A path that quietly sends a banner instead is a product bug, not a shortcut. Three did: the preview, snooze follow ups, and pinned occurrences folded into the plan.
Downgrades must explain themselves. Allowed only when the API genuinely cannot accept the date, and the reason is reported.

04 / The core design

Three tiers, chosen per habit

This is not cosmetic. Each tier is a different iOS API with a different system budget.

Tier API Buttons without long press Breaks Focus and silent Budget
gentle UNNotificationRequest no no 64 pending, shared
standard ActivityKit Live Activity yes, on Lock Screen no undocumented, assume tiny
alarm AlarmKit (iOS 26+) yes, always yes maximumLimitReached

standard exists for exactly one reason: the long press. alarm recurs natively, so a weekday 7 AM habit is one alarm rather than one request per day, and it never touches the 64 slot budget. Live Activity concurrency is undocumented, so a refusal is treated as routine and those nudges degrade to plain notifications.

05 / The constraint everything bends around

64 pending requests, app wide, system enforced

64pending local notification requests, for the whole app. No workaround. Confirmed by Apple DTS.
48 hrolling window the scheduler plans against, so slots are spent where they still matter.
3snoozes maximum, and a snooze actually brings the nudge back.
1 minprecision on every schedule: interval, fixed time, and X per day, week or month.
  • Allocation is fair, so one every 15 minutes habit cannot starve a once a day habit.
  • Collisions are queued across every tier at once, but the queue never affects delivery. Every nudge is pre scheduled with the OS at its staggered slot, so nothing depends on the app being alive.
  • sync diffs and adds before it removes. Clear then rebuild left a window with nothing scheduled, and an app suspended inside it fired nothing.

06 / Lockdown

Every other app is shielded until the habit is done

  • One tap on the block screen marks it done and gives the phone back.
  • shield.applicationCategories = .all(except: []) covers every category on the device. Psst is exempt automatically, which is what keeps the unlock reachable.
  • Alarm tier only. A shield raised for a gentle reminder is a mismatch between what you asked for and what you got.
  • Ninety minute ceiling, enforced by intervalDidEnd. A deleted habit or a crashed extension must never leave a phone bricked.
  • Home Screen, Settings, and anything in Screen Time's Always Allowed are never shieldable. This is a commitment device, not a cage.
PsstMonitor
Woken at the habit's time with the app not running. Raises the shield.
PsstShield
Draws the block screen.
PsstShieldAction
Owns the buttons.
Apple splits this across three processes and there is no way to collapse them. The extensions compile two files, not the shared layer: a shield that answers slowly is replaced by the system's generic blocker.

07 / The assistant

Plain language in, real mutations out

It can do anything you can do in the app, with a confirmation gate on anything destructive.

It sees your record, not just your sentence. Every request carries, per habit: the last 14 days daily, the last 12 weeks, the last 12 months, and the date of its oldest record.
Deeper history is one extra pass. The model calls fetch_history, the device resolves it against SwiftData and asks once more with the data attached. Exactly one retry, so a model that keeps asking cannot loop.
Destructive calls never apply directly. deleteHabit and clearRange surface a confirmation with a concrete count first.
The minimum interval is a code floor, not a prompt instruction. Enforced in SchedulingService.fireTimes, again in MutationApplier.apply, and again in the Worker's validateSchedule. A model cannot talk past it.

08 / Adaptation

It moves nudges to the hours you actually answer

ScheduleAdvisor is the piece that makes the app adaptive rather than merely configurable. It reads the response heatmap and returns at most one suggestion.

8answered nudges overall before it will speak at all.
3answers in a given hour before that hour counts as reliable.
1suggestion at a time, maximum. A suggestion rejected twice is worse than none.
1 tapto apply, from habit detail or the weekly review.

It will not propose tightening a window unless a reliably answered hour survives the trim. Suggestions are derived from when you actually answer, not when you said you would.

09 / Under the hood

Native, because the APIs are the product

Device
Swift 6 with SWIFT_DEFAULT_ACTOR_ISOLATION = MainActor, SwiftData, UserNotifications, ActivityKit, AlarmKit, FamilyControls, App Intents, WidgetKit.
ios/Shared/
Compiled into both the app and the widget extension: pure types, the intents, and the 64 slot planner.
Native Swift, not React Native.
A bridge would gate UNUserNotificationCenter, ActivityKit and AlarmKit, which are the product.
Worker
A Cloudflare Worker on D1 holds the API key and the conversation. Provider agnostic: Anthropic plus one OpenAI compatible adapter covering OpenRouter, Fireworks, DeepSeek, Groq and OpenAI.
MODEL_CHAIN
An ordered list of provider:model tried in turn. Escalation is driven by observed failure, never by predicting difficulty. A 4xx that is our own fault does not escalate, because the next model would receive the same malformed request.
Models chosen by measurement.
bun run eval runs real phrasings and checks the resulting mutations, including a floor that must not be undercut and a vague complaint that should be answered with a question.

The device owns the habits, the Worker owns the conversation. The app works offline; D1 mirrors habits only so a future push scheduler has something to read.
